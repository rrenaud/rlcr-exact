Brier score, decomposed

rlcr-exact · rlcr21 notes · 2026-11-26

Brier score is the mean squared error of a probability forecast: state a confidence c, observe y ∈ {0, 1}, pay (c − y)². A low score can come from two very different places. The forecaster can know more, telling likely successes from likely failures. Or it can be better calibrated, meaning its stated numbers match how often it is right. The Murphy decomposition separates the two exactly. That matters for deciding what to work on: changing how a model states its confidence can only fix the second.

The three terms

Group the rows by stated value v. Let nv be the number of rows that said v, ȳv their hit rate, and ȳ the overall hit rate. With bins equal to the stated values (our confidences come from a fixed list, so this is exact):

Brier = UNC − RES + REL UNC = ȳ (1 − ȳ) uncertainty: the score of always saying ȳ RES = Σ (n_v / N) (ȳ_v − ȳ)² resolution: how far the groups' hit rates spread from ȳ REL = Σ (n_v / N) (v − ȳ_v)² reliability: how far each stated value is from its group's hit rate
  • UNC is fixed by the task. No forecaster changes it. On HotpotQA dev the trained models are right about half the time, so UNC ≈ 0.249. NBA free throws go in 78% of the time, so UNC = 0.169.
  • RES is discrimination. It is the part of the variance of y that the forecast explains, the between-group variance in an analysis of variance. It goes up only when the forecast separates rows that differ in outcome. Relabelling the stated values leaves it unchanged, as long as rows that were grouped together stay together.
  • REL is miscalibration. It is zero when every stated value equals its group's hit rate. A monotone recalibration, a learned map from stated value to hit rate, can remove it without touching RES.

Two corrections make the terms usable on a 500-question dev set:

  • REL has a noise floor. Even a perfectly calibrated forecaster shows REL > 0 on finite data, because each group's hit rate is a sample. Its expected value is about Σv v(1 − v) / N. That is about 0.003 for 11 levels on 500 rows, and it doubles when the forecaster uses 21 levels. Only REL above that floor is evidence of miscalibration.
  • Cross-validated isotonic recalibration measures what fixing calibration is actually worth. Fit a monotone map from stated value to hit rate on 4/5 of the rows, apply it to the held-out fifth, and repeat over five folds. Its Brier is about the best any reshaping of the stated confidences can reach without new information.

A clean analogy: free throws

Forecast every free throw of the 2023–24 NBA regular season () using only the 2022–23 season. A free throw is a near-ideal test case. Its outcome is binary, the shooter is the only obvious predictor, and every attempt is equivalent: no defender, same distance.

  • League rate for everyone: say 0.78 on every shot. This is perfectly calibrated and has zero resolution, so Brier = UNC.
  • Each shooter's own rate last season, raw. A player who went 3 for 3 is forecast at 1.00. That costs calibration (REL 0.0015, about 8× its noise floor) for a little resolution.
  • Shrunk toward the league rate, using a beta prior fitted on 2022–23 alone ( pseudo-attempts). This keeps the same ranking as the raw rate, so RES is unchanged, and REL drops by 70%. Isotonic recalibration of the raw forecast lands in the same place. This is the lever "fix the calibration": it is real, and it is small.
  • Oracle, in-sample: each shooter's actual 2023–24 rate. This is not a forecast. It is the most that knowing the shooter can ever say, and Brier still only falls from 0.169 to 0.161. Everything else is shot-to-shot luck, and lowering it would take information about the individual shot.

Read the top block of the figure below that way. Free throws are a task where calibration is easy and discrimination is nearly impossible: RES is under 5% of UNC even for the oracle.

What our runs show

  • Before confidence training, Brier is all calibration. The Qwen base model and RLVR state confidences that are nearly unrelated to their hit rates: REL 0.27–0.32, more than the whole task's UNC. RL on the Brier reward fixes that almost completely. Released RLCR and all but two of the runs trained here sit within 0.008 of their REL noise floor. The two exceptions are sampled-arm runs (B0 seed 200, C0) at +0.015.
  • After confidence training, Brier is all discrimination. Trained runs differ from one another mainly in RES: 0.07 for released RLCR, 0.08–0.10 for ours. The seed-to-seed spread inside one config (about 0.015 Brier for A2 exact) is a RES spread. Cross-validated isotonic recalibration improves no exact-arm run by more than 0.006, and the two sampled exceptions by 0.015.
  • So a finer confidence grid cannot buy much. rlcr21 states confidences at 0.05 instead of 0.1. That only changes how calibration can be expressed, and calibration is already at its noise floor. It came out slightly worse in all three paired seeds, and the decomposition says why that was the likely outcome before the runs. Free throws show the opposite case. There, forecasts crowd into 0.6–0.9 and a 0.1 grid lumps together shooters who really differ, so the 11-level grid costs about 0.001 against 0.0004 for 21 levels. HotpotQA confidences span the whole range, so lumping costs much less.
  • Exact vs sampled is mostly a discrimination difference too, in the online stages. B2 exact reaches RES 0.086–0.095 against 0.069–0.076 for B0 sampled. C0 sampled also carries more REL (0.020 vs 0.007). These are single dev checkpoints, each arm at its own pick, so they illustrate the decomposition rather than replace the registered test comparison.
  • Questions are far more forecastable than free throws. Our trained runs remove 30–40% of UNC; the free-throw oracle removes 5%. The headroom is still in RES: information about whether this particular answer is right, such as agreement between sampled answers.
